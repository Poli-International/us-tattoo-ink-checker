## What is US Tattoo Ink Checker?

US Tattoo Ink Checker is a free browser-based screening tool that compares pasted tattoo ink ingredient text against a curated reference database of 15 chemical substances and heavy-metal classes. That curated set covers 22 CAS numbers and 10 Colour Index (CI) codes, and each entry is mapped to two US regulatory frameworks:

- **Federal MoCRA** (Public Law 117-328, the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022), which regulates tattoo inks as cosmetics and covers facility registration (§ 607), product listing (§ 607), 15-day serious adverse event reporting (§ 605), mandatory recall authority (§ 611), and labeling requirements (§ 609).
- **California Proposition 65** (California Health & Safety Code § 25249.5 et seq.), which requires a clear and reasonable warning before exposing anyone to a listed carcinogen or reproductive toxicant, unless exposure falls below an OEHHA-established Safe Harbor Level (an NSRL for carcinogens or a MADL for reproductive toxicants).

You paste text from an SDS Section 3, a bottle label, or an ingredient list. The tool matches it using four confidence types: exact CAS number match, Color Index (CI) match, word-boundary name match, and tokenized match. It then reports how many of the 15 curated substances were found, how many fall under the MoCRA framework, and how many are listed under Proposition 65.

The tool states plainly, in its own interface, that the FDA has not approved any color additive for intradermal injection, and that a negative screening result is not a legal compliance determination, FDA approval, or safety certification. All processing happens in the browser; nothing is transmitted to an external server.

## How to Use the US Tattoo Ink Checker

### Switch Interface Language and Theme

1. Use the **Interface Language** selector in the top navigation. Seven languages are available: English, Français, Italiano, Deutsch, Español, Nederlands, and Português.
2. Use the theme toggle (**Switch to dark theme** / **Switch to light theme**) to adjust contrast for your workspace.

### Paste and Screen Ingredient Text

1. Locate the field labeled **Paste Safety Data Sheet (SDS) Section 3, Bottle Labeling, or Ingredient Text**.
2. Paste your text into the box, which shows the placeholder: `Paste ingredients, chemical names, or CAS numbers (e.g. CAS 1333-86-4, CI 77891, Titanium dioxide, Lead, Nickel, Toluene)...`
3. Watch the character counter (`{count} characters entered`) to confirm your text was captured.
4. Click **Screen Ingredient Text** to run the match against the curated database.
5. Click **Clear Input** at any time to reset the field. If you click Screen with an empty field, the tool shows: "Please enter or paste ingredient text, chemical names, or CAS numbers to screen."

### Read the Screening Results Summary

The **Screening Results Summary** card reports:

- **Curated Reference Database: {count} substances**
- **Matched in Submitted Text: {count} of {total} ({percent}%)**
- **Subject to Federal MoCRA Framework: {count}**
- **Listed under California Proposition 65: {count}**

Two jump links appear here: **View MoCRA Details →** and **View Prop 65 Safe Harbors →**. If nothing matches, the tool states that no substances from the curated 15-item reference list were matched, and reminds you to verify that all ingredients on the bottle or SDS are accounted for.

### Review the MoCRA (Federal Law) Section

This section carries the badge **US Federal Law (Applies Nationwide)** and cites Public Law 117-328. It includes the FDA's official position: "The FDA has not approved any color additives for injection into the skin (tattooing or permanent makeup)." It then lays out five statutory pillars:

- **Facility Registration (§ 607)**, including the requirement for foreign facilities to designate a US agent.
- **Cosmetic Product Listing (§ 607)**, covering formulation, ingredients, color additives, and manufacturing facilities.
- **15-Day Serious Adverse Event Reporting (§ 605)**, via MedWatch Form FDA 3500A, with records retained 6 years (3 years for exempt small businesses).
- **Mandatory Recall Authority (§ 611)**.
- **Labeling Requirements (§ 609)**, including domestic contact information and professional-use statements.

Below this, the **Federal MoCRA Status for Matched Ingredients** table lists each matched substance with its FDA MoCRA status and statutory citation.

### Review the Proposition 65 (California) Section

This section carries the badge **California State Law Only** and cites California Health & Safety Code § 25249.5 et seq. It explains the warning duty for businesses with 10 or more employees, and defines Safe Harbor Levels as NSRLs for carcinogens and MADLs for reproductive toxicants.

The **California Proposition 65 Matches in Submitted Text** table shows, per matched substance:

- **Chemical Name & CAS**
- **Listed Toxicological Endpoints** (labeled **Endpoints**)
- **Verified Safe Harbor Level (OEHHA)**, shown as **NSRL** or **MADL**, or the note "No Safe Harbor Level established by OEHHA"
- **OEHHA Regulatory Citation**, with an **OEHHA Primary Source →** link

### Filter the Curated Substance List

The **Curated Screening Database (15 Substances / Classes)** table is sourced from FDA Cosmetics (MoCRA) and OEHHA Title 27 CCR.

1. Filter by jurisdiction using **All Jurisdictions**, **Federal MoCRA**, or **California Prop 65**.
2. Use the search field: `Filter curated substances by name, CAS, or CI...`
3. The counter shows **Showing {count} of {total}**.

### Print the Screening Report

1. Click **Print Report** in the header or **Print Screening Report** in the navigation.
2. The print stylesheet hides navigation, theme toggles, and buttons, and applies high-contrast monochrome typography.
3. The printed document includes the report title, subtitle, date, curated scope (15 core substances), summary metrics, matched substance tables, and statutory citations, plus the attribution line noting it is an informational screening, not a regulatory certification.
4. Choose your printer or "Save as PDF" in your system dialog to archive the report.

## Use-Case Examples

### Example 1: Screening a Black Ink SDS

You paste the SDS Section 3 text for a black ink that lists `Carbon Black`, `CAS 1333-86-4`, and `CI 77266`. The tool returns an **Exact CAS Number Match** for Carbon Black. The results summary shows one match under the MoCRA framework and one under Proposition 65. In the MoCRA table, the entry cites `FD&C Act § 607; 21 CFR 74.2052` and notes the FDA has not approved it for intradermal injection. In the Prop 65 table, the endpoint reads **Carcinogenicity**, listed February 21, 2003, with the safe harbor field showing "No Safe Harbor Level established by OEHHA."

### Example 2: Checking a White Pigment for Laser Removal Risk

You paste a white ink ingredient list containing `Titanium dioxide` and `CI 77891`. The tool matches Titanium Dioxide (airborne, unbound particles of respirable size). The MoCRA status cites `21 CFR 73.2575` as a color additive exempt from certification, subject to product listing § 607. The substance notes flag that Q-switched laser removal may cause paradoxical darkening through reduction of Ti(IV) to dark Ti(III), which is useful context to share with a client before treating light shades.

### Example 3: Screening a Suspected "Black Henna" Product

You paste a product ingredient list containing `p-Phenylenediamine` and `CAS 106-50-3`. The tool matches PPD. The MoCRA status reads "ILLEGAL for skin application," cites `FD&C Act § 601; FDA Consumer Updates on Tattoo Inks`, and notes the FDA explicitly warns that PPD is adulterated and prohibited for direct skin application in tattoos such as "black henna." The notes describe it as a severe allergen and contact poison that can cause blistering, permanent depigmentation, and lifelong sensitization.

## What the Tool Does Not Do

- It does not calculate nickel release rates from piercing needles, body jewelry, or tattoo grips.
- It does not validate mill certificates or alloy standards such as ASTM F136 or ISO 5832-3.
- It does not decode the full global spectrum of cosmetic ingredients outside its 15 curated substances.
- It does not check pigments against EU REACH Annex XVII entry 75 or the SVHC candidate list.
- It does not grant or confirm FDA approval, since the FDA does not pre-approve color additives for intradermal injection.
- It does not submit facility registrations or product listings to FDA Cosmetics Direct.
- It does not perform chemical laboratory analysis on physical ink bottles.
- It does not provide legal advice or binding compliance determinations.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Has the FDA approved any tattoo ink for injection into the skin?**
No. The tool states the FDA's own position: "The FDA has not approved any color additives for injection into the skin (tattooing or permanent makeup)." Under the FD&C Act as amended by MoCRA, tattoo inks are regulated as cosmetics through post-market adulteration provisions, not pre-market injection approval.

**What is MoCRA and how does it affect tattoo studios?**
MoCRA is Public Law 117-328, the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022. It requires facility registration (§ 607), cosmetic product listing (§ 607), 15-day serious adverse event reporting via MedWatch Form FDA 3500A (§ 605), and gives the FDA mandatory recall authority (§ 611), plus labeling requirements (§ 609). Studios that manufacture or process inks for US distribution may need to register; the tool notes foreign facilities must designate a US agent.

**When does Proposition 65 require a warning for tattoo ink in California?**
Businesses with 10 or more employees selling or using products in California must provide a clear and reasonable warning before exposing anyone to a listed carcinogen or reproductive toxicant, unless exposure is demonstrably below OEHHA Safe Harbor Levels. Where no safe harbor level exists, any detectable exposure requires a warning unless proven insignificant.

**Why is Carbon Black listed under California Proposition 65?**
Carbon Black (CAS 1333-86-4) was listed on February 21, 2003 for carcinogenicity, specifically as airborne, unbound particles of respirable size. In liquid ink dispersions the particles are suspended, but handling raw dry pigment powders or aerosolized spray generates respirable particles subject to warning duties. OEHHA has not established a Safe Harbor Level for it.

**Can titanium dioxide in tattoo ink cause problems during laser removal?**
Titanium Dioxide (CI 77891) is used in opaque white and pastel shades and is listed for carcinogenicity (airborne, unbound respirable particles) as of September 2, 2011. The tool notes that laser removal with Q-switched lasers may cause paradoxical darkening through reduction of Ti(IV) to dark Ti(III).

**Is PPD allowed in tattoo ink under federal law?**
No. p-Phenylenediamine (PPD) is flagged in the tool as "ILLEGAL for skin application." The FDA explicitly warns that PPD is adulterated and prohibited for direct skin application in tattoos such as "black henna." It is described as a severe allergen and contact poison that can cause blistering, permanent depigmentation, and lifelong sensitization.

**How does the tool decide a substance is a match?**
It uses four confidence types: Exact CAS Number Match, Color Index (CI) Match, Word-Boundary Name Match, and Tokenized Match. This means pasting a CAS number, a CI code, or a chemical name can each trigger a match.

**What is the difference between an NSRL and a MADL?**
The tool defines Safe Harbor Levels as No Significant Risk Levels (NSRLs) for carcinogens and Maximum Allowable Dose Levels (MADLs) for reproductive toxicants. Where OEHHA has not established a safe harbor level, any detectable exposure requires a warning unless proven insignificant.

**Does my pasted ingredient text leave my device?**
No. The tool's disclaimer states that records generated are processed entirely in the browser and are never transmitted to any external server. Clearing the input, reloading the page, or closing the tab removes the entered text.

**Is a negative screening result a compliance clearance?**
No. The tool states that a negative screening result does not constitute a legal compliance determination, FDA approval, or safety certification, and that a negative result does not constitute regulatory clearance. It screens a curated body-art subset of 22 CAS numbers and 10 Colour Index codes.
